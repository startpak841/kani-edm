Animation That Reached Theaters in Korea, Vietnam, and China —
Episode Company

Co-producing with Korea opens up new opportunities. Korea’s support programs are open primarily to Korean companies, but when international producers, studios, and platforms form co-production partnerships with Korean companies, they can build projects that draw on both Korea’s production capabilities and its government support programs. Support programs designed around collaboration with international partners are already in place. The K-Animation Global Showcase in Canada, where you can see this structure firsthand, takes place November 3–4 in Toronto, and pre-registration is closing soon.

Our final featured studio is Episode Company, a KOSDAQ-listed animation production company that has released films theatrically in Korea, Vietnam, and China, with its own in-house 3D studio and global distribution partnerships. Listed in 2019, the company has also received the Presidential Award in the character category at the Korea Content Awards. We hear about its experience working with international partners and the partners it hopes to meet in Toronto.

THE INTERVIEW

with Kyu‑won Lee, Manager, Episode Company

Your films have been released theatrically in Korea, Vietnam, and China. What differed from market to market, and how did you work with local partners?

Kyu‑won Lee: We found that even the same film needs to be introduced differently to audiences in each market. In Korea, we can draw on the characters’ existing recognition, but overseas it’s important to communicate the appeal of the characters and story clearly from the start.

Drawing on our local partners’ market experience, we coordinated everything from dubbing and subtitles to promotional materials and release strategy, aiming to introduce each film in a way that fit its market.

 

What have you learned from building and maintaining global distribution partnerships with distributors, broadcasters, and digital platforms?

Kyu‑won Lee: It’s important to propose content that fits each partner’s target audience and distribution model, and to clearly agree on the scope of rights and the materials we’ll provide.

Even after a deal is signed, we support partners with the materials they need for localization and promotion and stay in regular contact, building trust over the long term.

 

From kids’ TV series such as Carrie and Super Kola: The School and Little Carrie’s Big Question to theatrical features, your in-house 3D studio has a wide range of production experience. How does that become a strength in international collaboration?

Kyu‑won Lee: With the Carrie kids’ series, we aimed to combine fun with educational value, and through our theatrical features we gained experience in sustaining longer narratives and conveying their emotions.

With The Shadow, made for teen and adult audiences, we’ve broadened our range of expression. Building on this experience, we can offer international partners titles suited to a wide range of audiences.

The Shadow: a fantasy K‑pop musical revenge story about beauty and desire.

What do you think will resonate with international audiences and partners in The Shadow, a K‑pop musical revenge story?

Kyu‑won Lee: The Shadow combines the themes of beauty and desire with K‑pop music and performance.

We’re especially confident in the appeal of the film’s soundtrack, and we expect the songs, which carry the story’s emotions, to leave a strong impression on international audiences and generate buzz alongside the film.

“We’re especially confident in the appeal of the film’s soundtrack.”

Kyu‑won Lee

Manager, Episode Company

 

What do international producers and platforms gain by co-producing with a Korean studio, and what kinds of global sales agents, distributors, and investors do you hope to meet in Toronto?

Kyu‑won Lee: We believe the strengths of working with a Korean studio are meticulous quality control and responsible production management. Communicating closely throughout production and managing schedules and quality together can build trust with international partners.

In Toronto, we’d like to meet global sales agents and distributors who understand the appeal of The Shadow and can work with us on regional theatrical releases and platform distribution.
