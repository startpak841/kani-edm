25+ Years of Production Expertise, a Genre-Spanning Lineup —
Studio Animal

Korea’s animation industry is building new projects by pairing its in-house production capabilities with diverse source IP. Those capabilities weren’t built overnight.

This issue features Studio Animal, a development and production studio that has been producing work across a wide range of formats and styles since it was founded in 2000 — more than 25 years. Its current slate spans genres and formats side by side, from the 52-episode TV series Another World Gukbap Master to the darkly comic psychological drama EURECA, the horror-thriller Nine Mountain: Paraghost, and the comedy-fantasy series Princess Hurricane. Of these, EURECA and Princess Hurricane are looking for co-production partners and investors. The ability to develop for multiple genres and audiences at once comes from years of production experience. We talk with the studio about how everything it has learned over those years shaped its current pipeline, and the kind of partners it hopes to work with.

THE INTERVIEW

with Jung‑Jin Hong, Creative Producer, Studio Animal

Since its founding in 2000, Studio Animal has spent more than 25 years producing work across a wide range of formats and styles. Of everything you’ve learned about production along the way, what has shaped your current pipeline the most?

Jung‑Jin Hong: It’s the confidence that we can build the right pipeline for any production concept and run it reliably. Our greatest asset is probably trust: not only our in-house team but also our outside partners believe in the projects we lead and are ready to support them.

 

Your lineup spans the 52-episode TV series Another World Gukbap Master, the feature films EURECA and Nine Mountain: Paraghost, and the series Princess Hurricane. How do you plan and run a slate like this all at once?

Jung‑Jin Hong: Each project is at a different stage of development or production, so we can balance staffing and schedules across projects in-house. Most of our projects also have co-production partners in Korea, and we run multiple projects by splitting responsibilities with them. In particular, what matters most to us is bringing on a director suited to each project, someone who can own it from start to finish.

 

You’ve branched out into black comedy, horror-thriller, action, and fantasy. How do you decide which genres to take on, and how do you factor in demand from international markets?

Jung‑Jin Hong: Most of our projects take on new concepts that have never been tried before — if not anywhere in the world, then at least in Korea. Naturally, we look for concepts with a clear hook and plenty of room to grow creatively. The business side matters too, but we weigh it only once we’re sure the core concept is fun.

Rather than chasing broad international demand, we look for the gaps in it. Then we develop the genres and projects we can execute best, given how financing and production work in Korea.

EURECA: Seoul, 2007. Four ten-year-olds start a secret society.

EURECA is a distinctly Korean story set in Seoul in 2007. How do you hope it will land with international audiences?

Jung‑Jin Hong: We wanted to show, in a way that is funny but bittersweet, how children’s natural urge to play gets warped and bursts out under the competitive, high-pressure education system they face in Korea. In recent years, children in many countries have come under growing pressure to compete. The details may differ a little, but we hope the film will strike a chord with audiences abroad.

“Rather than chasing broad international demand, we look for the gaps in it.”

Jung‑Jin Hong

Creative Producer, Studio Animal

 

EURECA and Princess Hurricane are both looking for co-production partners and investors. What kind of collaboration are you hoping for?

Jung‑Jin Hong: Pre-production on EURECA is nearly complete, and a first pass of about 30 minutes is already done. We’re looking for co-producers who can join a Harmony-based pipeline, or partners who can invest as part of a distribution deal. It’s a modestly budgeted animated feature, and we’re working to raise 30–40% of the production budget from outside Korea.

Princess Hurricane is in early development, with only a pilot film produced so far, so co-production is open from pre-production onward, script included. The project is based on appealing source material with a clear story, so we expect communication between co-production partners to be relatively easy. Our goal is to raise 50% of the production budget from outside Korea.
