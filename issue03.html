From Webtoon to Animated Series —
Studio W.Baba’s Elixir of the Sun

Proven stories open up bigger opportunities in animation. According to the 2025 Webtoon Industry Survey by the Korea Creative Content Agency (KOCCA), revenue in Korea’s webtoon industry reached KRW 2.29 trillion in 2024, up 4.4% from the previous year. Stories and characters that have already proven themselves with readers expand into animation and create new opportunities.

This issue features Studio W.Baba. Based on a web novel and webtoon, its East Asian–⁠inspired romance fantasy Elixir of the Sun has found readers overseas, too — a sign that a story born in Korea traveled abroad even before reaching the screen. As the market grows, the ability to carry an original fanbase onto the screen matters more than ever. With production support from KOCCA, the studio is bringing fans’ expectations to the screen using Unreal Engine 5 and AI tools. We hear how the original’s fanbase and a Korean team’s visual interpretation come together.

THE INTERVIEW

with Inchan Park, CEO / Executive Producer, Studio W.Baba

Why do you think international readers responded to the original Elixir of the Sun?

Inchan Park: We think readers overseas also connected with the story of Bayan, who breaks away from the environment that rejected her, chooses her own destiny, and grows through her relationship with Emperor Dan.

Another draw is that audiences can enjoy romance and drama together, set in an East Asian–⁠inspired world amid the power struggles of the imperial court.

 

Bayan and the cursed Emperor Dan are bound by a fated imprint. How are you bringing their relationship to the screen?

Inchan Park: We’re focusing on the emotional shift as Bayan and Dan come to mean something special to each other.

We want to express the imprint that binds them and their moments of healing through their expressions, glances, and pacing, so viewers can feel the emotional arc of the original on screen as well.

 

How are you using Unreal Engine 5 and AI tools to work more efficiently while keeping creative control?

Inchan Park: We plan each shot based on the director’s notes and storyboards, and we use Unreal Engine 5 and AI tools to streamline visualization and revisions.

Shared references and shot-by-shot prompts communicate our production intent, while our team reviews and revises the results and makes the calls on each scene’s emotion and direction.

Elixir of the Sun follows Bayan, a girl cast out by her village, who becomes bound to a cursed emperor, Dan, by a fated imprint and carves out her own destiny amid conflict at the imperial court.
▶ Watch the official trailer

Elixir of the Sun is a co-production supported by KOCCA production funding. How is the co-production structured, and how do you work with your partners?

Inchan Park: It’s a three-company co-production: Studio W.Baba leads animation production, DCC ENT handles the original IP, business, and global distribution, and VIVRAVO handles sound and dubbing.

Building on KOCCA’s production support, we bring together each company’s expertise, and we’re also in discussions with partners who could take part in international distribution and production investment.

“We want viewers to feel the emotional arc of the original on screen as well.”

Inchan Park

CEO / Executive Producer, Studio W.Baba

 

Which partners do you most want to meet in Toronto, and what kind of collaboration would you like to propose?

Inchan Park: We’d like to meet OTT platforms and distributors in North America that understand romance fantasy and female audiences.

Focusing on licensing and pre-sales, we hope to discuss partnerships that extend to English localization, joint marketing, and production investment.
