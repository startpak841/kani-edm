Studio Shelter’s Brew & Banish cafe

The Korean government offers dedicated production support for animation projects that draw on IP from other media, with up to about KRW 1.5 billion in funding available. In effect, policy is backing the work of turning webtoons, web novels, games, and other stories audiences already love into animation. This support also opens a point of collaboration for international partners working with Korean studios, as original IP, Korean production capabilities, and government support can all come together in a single project.

This issue features Studio Shelter, a studio at the center of this movement. Shelter is a 2D and 3D animation and webtoon studio with experience expanding existing IP into animation, including Maru is a Puppy, a family comedy based on a NAVER WEBTOON original about a puppy who becomes a five-year-old girl after making a wish on a star, and Dear My Hero, based on the MapleStory game IP. In Toronto, it will present Brew & Banish cafe, an original 2D supernatural action comedy series of 13 episodes, 22 minutes each, that combines Korean mythology, modern Seoul, and the energy of K‑culture. We hear about a Korean studio’s skill in bringing proven originals to life on screen, and the direction behind an original project made for global audiences.

THE INTERVIEW

with Jungwoo Yang, CEO, Studio Shelter

When you adapt a popular NAVER WEBTOON like Maru is a Puppy into animation, how do you distinguish between what readers of the original want you to keep and what you add on screen?

Jungwoo Yang: The characters’ personalities and relationships, along with the original’s distinctive humor and emotional tone, are the core elements we must preserve. In Maru is a Puppy, what matters is the puppy-like honesty and behavior Maru shows even in human form. In animation, we bring that charm to life by adding movement, voices, music, and the timing of each performance. When we add something new, we judge it by whether it conveys the original’s appeal even better.

 

What qualities of a beloved original do you think will resonate with global family audiences, too?

Jungwoo Yang: We believe the joy of living with a pet, caring for family, and relationships with neighbors resonate across cultures. Children laugh at Maru’s quirky antics, while adults connect with the feelings of the people who care for her. Comedy carried by facial expressions and movement is a particular strength, because audiences can enjoy it together whatever language they speak.

 

What does Shelter’s “proven production pipeline” look like in practice?

Jungwoo Yang: At its core is the close collaboration between a directing team that has worked together for years and our 2D and 3D production teams. Our directors are involved from early development through storyboards and animation to final compositing, keeping each project’s direction and quality consistent from start to finish. In terms of process, we set the direction for the story and design, check the flow and timing of each scene with storyboards and animatics, and then move into animation, backgrounds, and compositing. With Maru is a Puppy, we focused on consistency across a series, and with Dear My Hero, on action and the kind of polish the big screen demands. We are now bringing that experience to our new projects.

Brew & Banish cafe is an original 2D supernatural action comedy series set in a hanok café in modern Seoul, blending Korean mythology with the energy of K‑culture.

Brew & Banish cafe is an original series that combines Korean mythology, modern Seoul, and K‑culture. What inspired this combination, and how do you hope international viewers will respond?

Jungwoo Yang: The starting point was the idea that connecting the imagination of Korean mythology with everyday life in today’s Seoul could make for fun comic action. Modern Seoul is a city where tradition and new culture live side by side. We felt that setting supernatural events in a familiar space like a café could naturally connect the characters’ everyday lives with a much bigger adventure.

Korean mythology gives the world and its characters their individuality, while Seoul adds a lived-in feel and a sense of place. We see the rhythm and energy of K‑culture as the element that brings the action and the overall mood of the series to life.

We hope international viewers will enjoy the characters’ humor and action and become curious about Korean mythology and Seoul, too. Our goal is for viewers who don’t know much about Korean culture to step into the story easily, and to come away eager for these characters’ next adventure.

Jungwoo Yang

CEO, Studio Shelter

 

Among investors, streaming/OTT platforms, broadcasters, and distributors, who do you most want to meet in Toronto?

Jungwoo Yang: Our first priority is streaming/OTT platforms and broadcasters that understand the project’s target audience and personality and can connect it with international viewers. We especially hope to meet partners who have experience in animation for teen audiences and can discuss the project’s direction with us from the development stage.

Investors and distributors are just as important, because we need both the funding to make production happen and a path to bring the series to audiences. We hope to build partnerships that combine Shelter’s directing and production strengths with our partners’ market experience, so we can grow this project, and the ones that follow, together.
