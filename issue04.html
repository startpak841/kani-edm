A Korean mecha prototype sold out in Japan

GODBRAVE’s HUNTING HAWK

Korean IP is reaching beyond Asia and into North America. Japan accounts for the largest share of Korea’s webtoon exports at 49.5%, followed by North America at 21.0%. This shows that North America has become a core market for Korean IP. For studios with a proven fanbase and experience working with international partners, co-production in North America becomes much more attainable.

This issue features GODBRAVE, a Korean studio that has already connected with fans overseas. Founded by the creators behind transforming robot toys that have sold more than one million units, the studio saw the prototype of its original IP HUNTING HAWK sell out at Wonder Festival in Japan. Using a hybrid pipeline that layers 2D hand-drawn art over 3D CGI, GODBRAVE is creating Korea’s first original mecha feature film for adult audiences, with a three-minute pilot to be produced in December 2026 and a theatrical release targeted for 2029. We hear how the studio has worked with international partners, and how that experience can become a strength in North American collaborations.

THE INTERVIEW

with Manho Seo, Vice President, GODBRAVE

How did your experience designing transforming robot toys that have sold more than one million units carry over into developing an original mecha IP and animation?

Manho Seo: We genuinely love transforming robots. The transforming robot toys we made with that passion have sold more than one million units, and along the way we came to understand, better than anyone, what this market and its fans want. HUNTING HAWK is designed around the stories and characters those fans want to see.

 

Your 185mm prototype sold out at Wonder Festival in Japan. What was the most meaningful thing you saw in the response from fans overseas?

Manho Seo: When we unveiled our 185mm prototype at Wonder Festival in Japan, every unit we had prepared sold out on site. What meant the most was that even though it was an original Korean robot they had never seen before, Japanese mecha fans tried out the combining mechanism for themselves and immediately reached for their wallets. It showed us that fans see the quality of the engineering before they see where it comes from.

 

What have you learned from working with international partners like Good Smile Company, and how do you work with global toy and IP partners?

Manho Seo: We have been designing and releasing a range of plastic model kits with Good Smile Company since 2020. What we’ve learned from working with international partners is to make sure everyone speaks the same language on design data, schedules, and quality standards from the very start. We work with clearly divided roles: we handle planning, design, and engineering, while our partners handle production and distribution.

HUNTING HAWK: Predatory Union is Korea’s first original mecha feature film for adult audiences.

The combining gimmick works the same way in the animation as it does in the actual toy, and you handle the entire toy production process in-house. How does this structure benefit international co-production partners?

Manho Seo: Because the combination you see on screen works exactly the same way in the actual toy, the animation becomes the blueprint for the product line. We handle everything ourselves, from planning and engineering to tooling and mass production, so co-production partners don’t need to align the film and the products separately. They can plan the business as a single franchise from the start.

“Because the combination you see on screen works exactly the same way in the actual toy, the animation becomes the blueprint for the product line.”

Manho Seo

Vice President, GODBRAVE

 

With a three-minute pilot coming in December 2026 and a theatrical release planned for 2029, what kinds of co-production partners, investors, and distributors are you hoping to meet in Toronto?

Manho Seo: We’re preparing a three-minute pilot for December 2026, followed by a theatrical release in 2029. In Toronto, we hope to meet distributors and streaming platforms that can bring the adult mecha genre to North American audiences, co-production partners to make the feature with us, and investors interested in a franchise that extends all the way to toys.
