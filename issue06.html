The Microbe Guests of a 5,000-Year-Old Hotel —
WeNobs’s Hotel Penni

Yoon Sang Eum, Director of the Content IP Business Division at the Korea Creative Content Agency (KOCCA), says Korean animation has grown beyond outsourced production into an industry with its own creative development capabilities and IP. The Korean government continues to expand its support in step with this shift.

This issue features WeNobs, a Jeju-based creative studio whose work spans original IP development, animation, VR/AR, and immersive media — an example of that shift. Hotel Penni, an original IP set in a 5,000-year-old hotel where the guests are microbes, is a 13 x 11′ edutainment series for kids 5–8, premiering on SBS in Korea on November 4. The studio is also planning to extend the IP into publishing, education, character merchandise, and licensing. We talk with CEO Hwa Jeong Lee about how a single original IP grows from a broadcast series into a wider business, and what it means to work with a Korean studio that develops its own original IP. The Toronto showcase is where international partners can meet the team behind it in person.

THE INTERVIEW

with Hwa Jeong Lee, CEO, WeNobs

What led you to develop original IP from Jeju, and where did the idea for a hotel full of microbes come from?

Hwa Jeong Lee: WeNobs has been making content in Jeju for 17 years. Formed by volcanic eruptions, Jeju was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as a natural site in 2007 as “Jeju Volcanic Island and Lava Tubes.” Its unique natural environment holds many stories, and we draw on them to develop original IP that can travel globally.

Hotel Penni began with a question: wouldn’t the tiny, invisible microbes that have supplied Earth with oxygen and sustained life for 3.6 billion years need a rest, too? It’s an animated series about comfort, empathy, and kindness that adults and kids can enjoy together.

 

Season 1 (13 episodes) premieres on SBS this November. What has the production process looked like from development to broadcast, and what does airing on SBS in Korea mean for international sales?

Hwa Jeong Lee: Since we began development in September 2024, we’ve created the scripts, characters, backgrounds, and designs step by step, and we’re producing the series using a hybrid 2D/3D approach.

Its SBS premiere on November 4, 2026, falls during the showcase in Canada. Airing on a major Korean network gives the series credibility — and a chance to prove its quality and market potential to international buyers and partners.

 

What’s your roadmap for expanding into publishing, education, character merchandise, licensing, and lifestyle products after Season 1?

Hwa Jeong Lee: Alongside the Season 1 premiere, we plan to release sing-along videos featuring the characters on YouTube.

Building on the show’s unique microbe theme, we’ll expand into early-childhood educational materials and board games, and we’re also exploring collaborations with international publishers. After that, we plan to extend into character licensing and lifestyle products.

How do you plan to use your VR/AR and immersive content capabilities to expand the animated IP?

Hwa Jeong Lee: We’ll draw on the VR/AR and immersive content expertise we’ve built — including the VR animations Viva Forest and Happyrus, and media art for Korea’s national heritage — to build out the world of Hotel Penni.

We’ll develop hands-on experiences in which children step inside the microbe hotel to meet the characters and explore, and present them at science museums, exhibition spaces, and educational settings. The goal is an immersive IP experience that takes kids from watching Hotel Penni to stepping inside it.

Among distributors, streamers, broadcasters, and licensees, who do you most want to meet in Toronto?

Hwa Jeong Lee: The partners we most want to meet are producers who can team up with Canadian children’s broadcasters on co-productions. Canada has a strong track record in kids’ animation co-productions, so we hope to find partners to create future seasons with us.

We’d also like to meet distributors who can help us into North American markets and onto streaming platforms, as well as licensees in education and publishing, to explore taking Hotel Penni global together.
